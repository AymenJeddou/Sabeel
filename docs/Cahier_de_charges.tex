\chapter{Introduction and context}
% =====================================================================
\section{Problem statement}
Tunisia has roughly 20{,}000~km of classified roads (national, regional, local) plus thousands
of kilometres of city streets. Their surface degrades quickly for three reasons:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Heat} --- summers above 45\,\textdegree C followed by heavy autumn rain crack the surface.
\item \textbf{Water} --- water enters the cracks and weakens the base layer; a pothole can appear within days.
\item \textbf{Slow, unstructured reporting} --- potholes are reported by phone, letter or social media,
with no common format, no exact location and no follow-up.
\end{itemize}
\why{A pothole repaired early costs far less than one left for months. The fastest lever on cost is
therefore to shorten the delay between ``pothole appears'' and ``repair order issued''. This is the single
idea RoadScan implements.}

\section{Existing solutions and positioning}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{4cm}L{4.6cm}L{5.2cm}@{}}
\toprule\textbf{Approach} & \textbf{Strength} & \textbf{Limitation}\\\midrule\endhead
Survey vehicles with cameras & Accurate & Expensive, low refresh frequency\\
Generic complaint apps & Cheap, easy & No automatic grading, no structured output\\
Research AI detectors & Good detection & Stop at detection, no repair order\\
\textbf{RoadScan} & Crowd reporting + automatic severity grading + rule-grounded work sheet
& Depends on citizen adoption; MVP scope\\\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
RoadScan combines the low cost of crowd reporting, the automatic grading of AI detectors and, as its
differentiating step, converts a GPS point into a work sheet that \textbf{quotes the applicable legal rule}
through a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) agent.

\begin{center}\itshape photo $\rightarrow$ score $\rightarrow$ deduplicated map dot $\rightarrow$
rule-cited work sheet $\rightarrow$ routed to the right office $\rightarrow$ status tracked until fixed.\end{center}

\section{F1 --- Photo capture and report}
\textit{As a citizen, I take a photo of a pothole and the app captures my exact position and grades it
without me typing anything.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The app sends: photo, \texttt{lat}, \texttt{lon}, \texttt{gps\_accuracy\_m}, \texttt{speed\_kmh},
\texttt{direction\_deg}, \texttt{captured\_at} (UTC), \texttt{source} and a phone-generated \texttt{device\_report\_id}.
\item The vision model returns a whole-number \texttt{score} 1--5 and a \texttt{confidence} in $[0,1]$.
\item Above about 40~km/h a manual photo is likely blurred; it is flagged for review rather than rejected.
\item Offline, the report is stored in a local queue and uploaded later; the \texttt{device\_report\_id} is never regenerated.
\item Without GPS permission the app explains why the permission is needed.
\end{itemize}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{1.2cm}L{4cm}L{2cm}L{6.4cm}@{}}
\toprule\textbf{Score} & \textbf{Meaning} & \textbf{Colour} & \textbf{System behaviour}\\\midrule\endhead
1 & Small crack / hole & Green & Counted only\\
2 & Low severity & Green & Counted, alert if clustered\\
3 & Medium & Orange & Sheet generated once confirmed\\
4 & High & Orange & Sheet generated, repair soon\\
5 & Serious / dangerous & Red & Sheet generated, urgent\\\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\textbf{Implementation note.} The score is computed by \texttt{score\_from\_detections()} in
\texttt{ml-training/severity\_scoring.py} from the \emph{relative area} of the largest confident bounding box
(thresholds 0.5\,\%, 1.5\,\%, 4\,\%, 8\,\% of the image), plus one level for confidence $>0.85$. Depth in
millimetres cannot be measured from a single 2-D photo (decision AD-4).

\textbf{Acceptance.} (1) Photo and location reach the API in one request; (2) \texttt{score}
$\in\{1,\dots,5\}$, \texttt{confidence} $\in[0,1]$; (3) an offline report is delivered exactly once after
reconnection; (4) open app $\rightarrow$ report sent in $<15$~s.

\textbf{Status:} \sPart{} --- capture, preview and result screens, GPS/heading reading and the offline-queue
repository exist in \texttt{mobile/lib}; the API stores reports and runs ONNX inference. Automatic queue flushing is still to be wired.

\section{F2 --- Map with filters and deduplication}
\textit{As a city officer, I open a map, see problem spots at a glance and never see the same pothole twice.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Full-screen OpenStreetMap; each pothole is a dot coloured by score.
\item A new report within \textbf{5~m} of an existing one shares its \texttt{cluster\_id} and the state moves from
\texttt{new} to \texttt{confirmed}.
\item The proximity test runs \textbf{in the database} (PostGIS \texttt{ST\_DWithin} on a GiST index), never on the phone.
\item \texttt{GET /map} returns the reports in the current viewport; tapping a dot shows the record.
\item Filters by score, status and region; marker clustering by zoom level for 5{,}000+ points.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Acceptance.} Two reports 3~m apart share a \texttt{cluster\_id} and are \texttt{confirmed}; two reports
30~m apart stay independent; the map stays fluid with 5{,}000+ points.

\textbf{Status:} \sPart{} --- deduplication and \texttt{GET /map?bbox=} are done; the map screen exists;
\texttt{sev}/\texttt{state} filters and marker clustering are planned.

\section{F3 --- Work-sheet generation (RAG)}
\textit{As a technical manager, a confirmed serious pothole becomes a real work order routed to the right
office with the applicable rules quoted.}

\textbf{Trigger.} Automatic, when \texttt{status = confirmed} \emph{and} \texttt{score} $\geq$
\texttt{SCORE\_THRESHOLD\_FOR\_SHEET} (default 3). No user action.

\textbf{Processing (RAG service).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Determine the road type (\texttt{urbain\_principal}, \texttt{urbain\_secondaire}, \texttt{rural}, \texttt{autoroute}, \texttt{piste}).
\item Retrieve the top-5 rule chunks from Chroma (multilingual embeddings), discarding matches below a similarity floor.
\item Build a prompt forcing the LLM to cite a rule identifier for every claim and to answer ``not enough rules'' when nothing matches.
\item Call the primary LLM (Llama~3.1~8B~Instruct), then the backup (Qwen~2.5~72B~Instruct), then a deterministic template if the output is invalid.
\item A citation guard rejects any citation absent from the retrieved set.
\item Return \texttt{sheet\_text}, \texttt{rule\_citations}, \texttt{target\_office}, \texttt{road\_type}.
\end{enumerate}
\why{A fixed template cannot adapt to the road type or quote the right rule; an unconstrained LLM can invent
rules, which is unacceptable in an official document. RAG restricts the model to real text and every line
stays traceable to its source.}

\textbf{Acceptance.} Every sheet cites at least one real, retrievable rule; \texttt{target\_office} is never
empty and belongs to \{Municipality, DGPC, Ministry of Equipment\}; a sheet is produced in $<20$~s; a
generation failure never blocks report creation (the report stays \texttt{confirmed}).

\textbf{Status:} \sPart{} --- pipeline, retrieval, citation guard, fallback and tests exist under
\texttt{rag-service/}; the corpus combines \texttt{rules\_real} (Law 2018-29, Law 86-17) with
\texttt{rules\_placeholder}. The current API integration is a synchronous HTTP call inside \texttt{POST /reports};
a background task is planned.

\section{F4 --- Priority list and status workflow}
\textit{As a city, I want a sorted list of what I must fix and a button to mark it done.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Each office sees only its own potholes, sorted by urgency $\times$ traffic $\times$ age.
\item Status moves strictly forward: \texttt{new} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{confirmed} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{sheet\_sent}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{fixed}; any other transition is rejected with \textbf{HTTP 409}.
\item Every change is time-stamped; the list is exportable as CSV / GeoJSON.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Status:} \sPart{} --- state machine and \texttt{PATCH /reports/\{report\_id\}} are done with tests;
the \texttt{report\_events} history table, per-office queue and exports are planned.

\section{F5 --- Citizen feedback}
\textit{As a driver, I want to know my report was seen and whether it has been fixed.}
No account: the phone stores the \texttt{device\_report\_id} of each report it created;
\texttt{GET /reports/\{device\_report\_id\}} returns \texttt{status}, \texttt{score}, \texttt{sheet\_summary};
a local notification appears when a tracked report becomes \texttt{fixed}.
\why{All data is donated by citizens; proof that reporting has an effect is the only lasting incentive.}
\textbf{Status:} \sPart{} --- endpoint done; ``My reports'' screen exists on mock data; polling and notification planned.

\section{F6 --- Hands-free driving mode}
\textit{As a driver, I put the phone on the windscreen and the app finds and reports potholes on its own.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Frames are sampled at a fixed low rate (target 1 frame/s, final value fixed after the Sprint~3 blur/battery test --- AD-8).
\item A pothole is reported only after \textbf{3 consecutive positive frames}.
\item Capture is suspended when the vehicle is stationary and above \textbf{80~km/h}.
\item Reports use \texttt{source = driving\_mode} and follow the same deduplication as F2.
\item Empty frames are discarded and never uploaded; full trips are never stored.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Acceptance.} 20+ minutes without crash or overheating; only confirmed detections uploaded; driving-mode
reports appear on the same map as manual ones. \textbf{Status:} \sPlan{} (Sprint~3).

\chapter{System architecture}
% =====================================================================
\section{Architectural overview}
RoadScan consists of \textbf{one mobile client and three logical services} behind a core API. The vision model
is called \emph{in-process} as a Python function. The RAG agent is a \textbf{separate HTTP service}, because its
dependencies (PyTorch, ChromaDB, sentence-transformers) are too heavy to share the Core API image. The whole
stack runs on free tiers and scales to zero.

\figwide{architecture}{Architecture overview}{fig:arch}
\figimg{component}{Component diagram: provided and required interfaces}{fig:component}
\figimg{deployment}{Deployment diagram}{fig:deployment}

Local development uses \texttt{docker-compose.yml} with three services (\texttt{db} = PostGIS~16, \texttt{api},
\texttt{rag-service}) and persistent volumes for the database and the Chroma store.

\textbf{Decision AD-1.} Earlier versions packaged all services in one container. The repository ships two
Dockerfiles (Core API + vision, and RAG) to keep PyTorch/Chroma out of the API image and its cold start.

\chapter{Data, privacy and legal compliance}
% =====================================================================
\section{Training data (WP1)}
Free datasets (Roboflow, Kaggle) plus team photos and driving clips from Tunisia; target 2{,}000+ images
(Sprint~1: $\geq400$ labelled), split 70/15/15 \textbf{by geographic area}. A single label, \texttt{pothole};
severity is \emph{derived} from the bounding box. Augmentation: blur, rain, shadow, brightness. Training:
YOLOv8n from pretrained weights, final run targeted at 60 epochs, \texttt{imgsz=640} (Sprint~1 pipeline verified with one epoch).

\section{Rule corpus (WP2)}
Law 86-17 (road classification), Law 2018-29 (Code des Collectivit\'es Locales) and technical references (to be
completed). Chunking is semantic: one chunk per article or clause; chunks above 300 words are split into
$\sim$200-word parts with $\sim$35-word overlap, capped at 1{,}200 characters. Every sheet stores the chunks it
used, so faults are fixed in the rules, not in the model.

\section{Personal data}
\begin{itemize}
\item No name, phone number or account identifier is collected; a report is tied only to the phone's \texttt{device\_report\_id}.
\item Faces and licence plates are blurred before a photo is persisted (\sPlan).
\item Compliance with \textbf{Law 2004-63}; location data describes the road, not a person's trips.
\item Raw photos are deleted once the sheet is produced (\sPlan).
\item Driving mode discards empty frames on the device and never stores whole trips.
\end{itemize}

\section{Branching workflow}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{main} (demo-ready) and \texttt{dev} (integration) are protected; no direct push.
\item Feature branches \texttt{feature/...}, \texttt{fix/...}, \texttt{chore/...} are created from \texttt{dev}.
\item Pull requests target \texttt{dev}, need at least one review and reference the issue (\texttt{Closes \#n}).
\item CI must be green before merge; \texttt{dev} merges into \texttt{main} only at the end of a sprint.
\end{itemize}
